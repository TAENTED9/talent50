## 3. The Problem — The Missing Middle

**Section label:** The opportunity

**Heading:** Strong at the top. Strong at the bottom. Hollow in between.

**Body:**
Africa produces world-class talent the world pays a fortune for — once it leaves. At the top sit marquee events and stars exported abroad. At the bottom sit 225M+ amateur footballers and the youngest population on earth. What's missing is everything in between: leagues, distribution, data, commercialisation and capital. Grassroots receives less than 5% of national sports budgets, and the sector runs on almost no fan data. That gap is the opportunity — and it's where we build.

**Three supporting points:**
- **Talent leaves early, cheap.** The average outbound transfer fee from Africa is a fraction of what the same player commands once they reach a top-tier league.
- **Value is captured abroad.** The platforms, the rights and the fan relationships are built somewhere else.
- **The home market is unbuilt.** 600M+ fans, almost entirely unmonetised — because no one has built the connective layer.

## 5. The Thesis — Mobile-First & Micro-Rights

**Section label:** Why now

**Heading:** Mobile-first consumption creates a new asset class: micro-rights.

**Body:**
Africa skipped legacy TV. Fans consume sport on their phones first — short-form, social, always-on. When a billion fans watch on mobile, attention fragments into millions of discrete, ownable moments. Each one can be rights-managed, licensed and monetised on its own. That granularity is the IP opportunity — and the reason distribution has to be mobile-first from day one.

**Micro-rights, four ways:**
- **Clip & moment rights** — goals, highlights and viral moments licensed individually across platforms.
- **Athlete NIL micro-licenses** — name, image & likeness unbundled into many small, recurring deals.
- **Format rights** — repeatable show and league formats owned and re-sold market by market.
- **Creator & fan rev-share** — co-created content with built-in splits, turning fans into distribution.

## 6. Market & Momentum (numbers strip)

**Section label:** The market

**Heading:** A continent-sized audience, almost entirely unmonetised.

- **$12B** — African sports market today, projected to surpass $20B by 2035.¹
- **600M+** — sports fans across the continent.
- **225M** — amateur footballers in Africa.²
- **<2%** — of athlete IP currently monetised.

**Momentum line:** Title-sponsored leagues jumped from under 15% in 2010 to over 70% in 2025.³ The rails are maturing: African sports betting reached ~$3B in 2025 (mobile-first), 350M+ gamers play mostly on mobile, and MEA sports streaming is on track to grow from $1.3B to $2.4B by 2030.⁴ Yet African sport still runs on almost no data infrastructure — and 320M mobile-internet subscribers are already on the network.

*Footnote: ¹ Oliver Wyman · ² ASCI · ³ PwC Africa Sports Outlook 2025 · ⁴ Grand View Research, Stears, SCCG, GSMA.*

## 7. How It Works — The Engine

**Section label:** The engine

**Heading:** School pitch to global feed — one compounding loop.

**Body:**
School sports bring in mass participation, scouting and jobs. Academies and leagues develop that talent in new, mobile-native formats. Local stars emerge — fuelled by the aspiration of African players abroad. Their stories become repeatable content and owned IP, which deepens our fan base. Those fans monetise, and the revenue compounds back into the funnel.

**Pull-quote:** We own the talent at the bottom of the funnel and the fan at the top — so every star we create funds the next one. The loop is the moat.
